## 1. Introduction  

Solid‑state batteries (SSBs) replace the flammable liquid electrolyte of conventional Li‑ion cells with an inorganic, polymeric or hybrid solid electrolyte.  The promise of SSBs—dramatically higher energy density, intrinsic safety, and the ability to pair lithium‑metal anodes with high‑voltage cathodes—has moved them from a long‑term research concept to a near‑term commercial target for electric‑vehicle (EV) manufacturers, grid‑scale storage, and portable electronics.  

During 2023‑2024 a confluence of factors accelerated progress:

* **Energy‑density pressure:** EV OEMs now demand > 500 Wh kg⁻¹ (cell) and > 800 Wh L⁻¹ (pack) to meet range‑and‑cost targets.  
* **Safety imperatives:** Solid electrolytes are non‑flammable and suppress thermal runaway up to ≈ 350 °C.  
* **Manufacturing breakthroughs:** Roll‑to‑roll tape‑casting, cold‑press densification and 3‑D printing now achieve > 95 % theoretical density at pilot‑scale.  
* **Policy & funding:** The U.S. DOE “Battery 2030+” program (US $2 B) and the EU Battery Alliance have earmarked billions for ASSB (all‑solid‑state battery) pilot lines.  

The following sections synthesize the most consequential technical advances reported in the last two years, grouped into three overarching themes that together define the current state‑of‑the‑art.
